Climate · Antalya

Summit runs overtime as the fossil-fuel words vanish

COP31 was due to close on Friday evening. It did not. A third draft of the Antalya text is promised today, after the second removed every mention of coal, oil and gas and dropped a $120bn adaptation target.

The conference opened in Antalya on 9 November for what was meant to be twelve days. By Friday evening the plenary chamber was still full and the deadline had gone: Murat Kurum, Turkey’s environment minister and president of COP31, adjourned the session with a promise that teams would work overnight on a third revision of his proposed decision.

Delegates did adopt technical decisions on Friday, including cooperation under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement. The wider political package — finance, emissions, adaptation, a just transition — stayed open.

At least 23 countries had objected to the missing fossil-fuel language. France and Panama led the demand that it be put back.

What changed between drafts is the story. Thursday’s version halved the text, deleted the $120bn adaptation figure in favour of a promise to “at least triple adaptation finance by 2035”, and pushed the detail into an “Antalya-to-Addis Ababa Roadmap”.

Chris Bowen, Australia’s climate minister, who leads the negotiations, argued that this COP was about implementation rather than reopening old commitments.

It also kept the presidency’s “35 by 35” goal — 35% of final energy consumed as electricity by 2035 — without saying the power must be clean, and leaned hard on carbon capture. For the first time, a COP text concedes that 1.5°C will be overshot.
